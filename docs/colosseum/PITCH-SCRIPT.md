# SettleKit pitch video script (target 2:40, hard limit 3:00)

Format: founder on camera, with screen inserts. Spoken lines are in quotes; `[ON SCREEN]` notes are for the edit. Read at a calm pace (about 150 words per minute). Total spoken text is about 400 words.

---

## 0:00 to 0:20 — Hook

"I build software products. An AI video tool called Menivor, a Postgres backend called Scribase, and a go-to-market tool called Rally. When it was time to charge for them, the merchant-of-record platforms said no. Creem turned us down. No appeal. No way to get paid."

[ON SCREEN] The three product logos, then a redacted rejection email.

## 0:20 to 0:45 — Problem

"This happens to founders, small teams and app studios every day. AI products, developer tools, sellers outside the US. Stablecoins move the money, but a wallet address is not a business. You still need a checkout, you need to know which payment belongs to which order, and you need to actually deliver access. And your buyers hold money on ten different chains."

[ON SCREEN] A wallet address in a tweet, then a messy spreadsheet of tx hashes.

## 0:45 to 1:30 — Solution

"SettleKit is a payment link for software that works on every chain. The seller creates a product, sets a price in dollars, and shares one link."

"The buyer pays in USDC on Solana, Base, Ethereum, Arbitrum or Hyperliquid, USDG on Robinhood Chain, USDC.e on Tempo, or ZEC on Zcash. Or they pay with any token from another chain, and it's routed into the seller's stablecoin."

"SettleKit verifies the transfer on-chain itself, and the moment it's confirmed, access is delivered: a GitHub repo invite, a license key, a Discord role, a SaaS plan."

"And the newest buyer is an AI agent. Agents buy over x402 on Solana and EVM chains, or MPP on Tempo, and get the product back in the same request."

[ON SCREEN] 3-second cuts from the demo: payment link, Solana Pay QR, any-token quote, Zcash QR, agent terminal, GitHub invite email.

## 1:30 to 1:55 — Why it's trustworthy

"Everything fails closed. If we can't verify a chain, we don't offer it. We never trust a bridge's status; we check that the money landed in the seller's wallet. One transaction pays one order. Payers are bound by signature. And every payment is public on our proof page, with explorer links, mainnet and testnet kept separate."

[ON SCREEN] `/proof` page scrolling.

## 1:55 to 2:15 — Business and traction

"We charge one percent per payment. No monthly fee. It's open source and self-hostable."

"Traction: [FILL: N] mainnet payments, [FILL: $X] volume across [FILL: N] chains, [FILL: N] agent purchases, all verifiable on the proof page. Our own products are the first sellers."

[ON SCREEN] Proof page totals with numbers highlighted. Only say numbers that `/proof` shows.

## 2:15 to 2:35 — Go-to-market

"We start with ourselves, then go to the sellers the merchants of record rejected. They're easy to find: they post about it. Then API and tool builders who want AI agents as customers, through the x402 and MPP ecosystems."

## 2:35 to 2:50 — Close

"Getting paid shouldn't depend on someone else's approval. SettleKit: get paid on any chain, deliver access automatically. Built by a founder who needed it."

[ON SCREEN] Logo, URL, GitHub repo URL.

## Recording notes

- Record the hook and close on camera; everything else can be voice-over on screen captures from the demo recording.
- Keep the traction line truthful. If mainnet numbers are small, say them anyway and point to `/proof`; do not quote testnet as volume.
- Describe the audience only as founders, small teams, app studios and software sellers.
- Do not claim shielded Zcash, native USDC on Robinhood Chain, or native USDC on Tempo.
